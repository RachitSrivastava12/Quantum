# Brand — Qubit Atlas

_Status: active_

Qubit Atlas is a self-paced quantum-computing course and browser lab. It guides curious learners from their first state vector to designing and analyzing small quantum-circuit experiments.

## Direction: Oxide & Graphite

Quiet, precise, and editorial. The visual language pairs warm mineral paper with charcoal ink and a restrained oxide accent. Typography should make technical material approachable without making the product feel playful or futuristic for its own sake.

### Core colors

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | `#F4F2EC` | Main canvas |
| Surface | `#FBFAF6` | Reading surfaces and raised panels |
| Ink | `#242521` | Primary text |
| Muted | `#5D6059` | Secondary copy, checked for readable contrast |
| Rule | `#D8D8D0` | Dividers and quiet outlines |
| Oxide | `#8D4936` | Primary accent, links, active states and focus |
| Oxide wash | `#EFE1DA` | Selected and highlighted surfaces |

## Typography

Use the system UI sans-serif stack for interface and body copy, Georgia for occasional editorial emphasis, and the system monospace stack for equations, circuit labels, and numerical output. This keeps the frontend self-contained and avoids requiring a font service.

## Voice

Use direct, careful explanations. Define technical terms before relying on them. Keep mathematical statements exact and distinguish ideal predictions, simulator samples, and physical hardware results. Avoid hype and avoid describing superposition as a way to read every answer at once.

## Usage

- Keep one accent color and use it for functional emphasis.
- Prefer thin rules, compact layouts, and square or lightly rounded corners.
- Use equations alongside plain-language explanation.
- Do not use rainbow gradients, glowing effects, cartoon illustrations, emoji, or oversized empty hero areas.
- Keep all lesson and experiment copy neutral and precise; do not use informal slang in the product UI.
